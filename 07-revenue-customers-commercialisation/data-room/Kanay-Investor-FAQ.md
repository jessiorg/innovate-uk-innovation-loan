# Kanay — Investor FAQ

*Top 20 questions from investor due diligence, with full responses.*
*Candid, no marketing gloss. Where a number is an estimate it is flagged ⚠️.*

*September 2026*

## A. Business model

### 1. What exactly is Kanay?

Kanay is an **Energy, Materials and Intelligence company**. We source, transform and supply the physical inputs of the new industrial economy — power, fuel, metals, magnets, compute — and we apply proprietary intelligence to make each transformation step capture more value than the one before it. The investor-side gloss is Proprietary Commodity Trading (Geneva/Merc comp path); the buyer-side gloss is Specialty Supplier (Materion / Vital Metals comp path). Both are honest descriptions of the same business. The detail is in the **Overview**.

### 2. How do you source deals?

Three pipelines in parallel. **Inbound** — producers (Elcogen, Magnotherm) approach Kanay because they need capital + offtake combination more than they need venture capital alone. **Outbound** — our venture studio identifies bottleneck technologies in the transformation ladder and initiates. **Co-invest** — strategic partners (infrastructure funds, family offices, sector majors) bring us opportunities they can't execute standalone. The Innovation Hub is the in-house venture studio; it graduates units to external revenue thresholds and feeds them into the integrated book.

### 3. What's the defensibility?

**First**, land-options on energy + compute sites ahead of need. **Second**, the bundled Source-Transform-Supply economics — three margins on the same electrons, which a pure-play at any single stage cannot replicate. **Third**, the proprietary applied-AI layer (KCC, the Kanay Classification System) which classifies feedstock chains, monitors transformation spreads, and prices asset utilisation in real time. None of these are easy to copy, and none are individually sufficient — the combination is the moat.

### 4. How do you scale?

By Year 3: **5 strategic stakes per year** (typical equity cheque $2–5M, taking 5–10%) and **5 offtake agreements per year** (typical 5–10-year tenor). Year 1 the cadence is lighter — 2 stakes + 2 offtakes — because operator recruitment precedes deal flow. Each stake + offtake bundles working-capital lines that produce immediate interest income. The integrated book is the scaling unit, not the individual deal.

## B. Competitive position

### 5. Why can't Glencore copy this?

Three structural reasons. **Cost-base lock-in.** Glencore's book is priced off replacement cost — what it would cost to find new supply. Their counterparty relationships, financing terms, and board mandates are built around that pricing model. Kanay prices off marginal cost of running assets we've already built. **Capital structure.** Renewable generation + GPU clusters + strategic stakes is a 25-year asset duration. Glencore is funded on 12-month working capital cycles. **Operational discipline.** Trading houses optimise for bid/ask spread; Kanay optimises for transformation-chain margin per MWh. The operating disciplines are different and don't port cleanly. Glencore could copy any single leg; they cannot copy the integrated chain without rebuilding their cost base.

### 6. Aren't you a trading house?

No. **Trading houses** market-make between external counterparties on standardised products. They don't own the assets or operate the transformation steps. **Kanay** owns the assets (generation, compute, stakes) and operates the transformation (tolling, installation, refining). Market-making is one tool in the kit; it is not the business. The comp set is split: integrated majors (ExxonMobil, Vitol) for the operating model; infrastructure funds (BlackRock, KKR) for the asset duration; specialty suppliers (Materion) for the customer-facing side.

### 7. What are you NOT?

Not a holding company — we run them. Not a venture fund — no GP carry, no fund vintage, no DPI obligation to LPs. Not a pure trading desk — assets are the platform, not the by-product. Not a tech company — we use AI as a capability, not as the product. Not a single-asset developer — we don't build renewables, we source them and run them as part of the integrated book.

## C. Capital & scaling

### 8. What does the capital structure look like?

Three layers. **Equity** (Series A in progress, target $25–50M) for stakes, working-capital lines, and first-of-a-kind asset capex. **Debt** (in discussion with infrastructure and growth-credit funds) for the long-dated asset base — GPU clusters, SOFC plants, cooling installations. **Counterparty finance** (prepay, offtake-receivable financing) for working-capital tied to physical flows. Target blended cost of capital is ~7–9% ⚠️; target return on equity is +20%. The 25-year duration of the underlying assets justifies a debt stack most trading houses cannot carry.

### 9. What is the use of funds?

| Allocation | % | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic equity stakes | 35% | 5–8 stakes/yr at $2–5M each |
| Working-capital lines | 20% | Prepay / offtake-receivable financing |
| Asset capex (GPU, SOFC, cooling) | 25% | First-of-a-kind installations |
| Applied AI / KCC buildout | 10% | Proprietary classification + intelligence |
| Operations + team | 10% | Trading desk, partnership team, ops/engineering |

### 10. What's the timeline to first material cashflow?

The Innovation Hub's ventures produce revenue from Month 6; working-capital lines produce interest income from Day 1. Material operating cashflow (~£10M annualised) is targeted by Month 18 with the first two stakes+offtakes live. Material compute cashflow follows at Month 24 with first H100 cluster online. The integrated portfolio in the **Overview** targets Year-3 net profit of $1.8B on the 100 MW blend — this is the steady-state target, not Year-1 run-rate.

### 11. How concentrated can any single product get?

**Soft-foot rule: no single product line exceeds 30% of book revenue.** The portfolio mix in the Overview respects this at video creation inference (currently 61.9% of net) — that ratio is acceptable because video inference is delivered through spot contracts and can be wound down inside a soft-foot window without stranding physical assets. The rule applies to **physical asset exposure** (compute, generation, refining) where stranding risk is real. Spot inference volume is excluded from the rule.

### 12. Anchor customer strategy?

Two anchor patterns. **Hyperscaler / large-industrial off-takers** for the compute and refining legs — credit-quality counterparties on multi-year SLAs, priced inside the book. **Distributed SME offtake** for the EV charging and cooling legs — diversified counterparty risk, retail margin captured. We do not depend on a single anchor. Loss of any one anchor is contained to that leg; the book is diversified.

## D. Risk & resilience

### 13. What if GPU pricing drops 30%?

Sensitivity is run on the integrated book in **Transformation Ladder §4**. A 30% GPU price decline moves blended payback from 0.30 to 0.36 years and blended net margin from 43% to 38%. The book remains profitable and sub-3-year payback. Long-dated hyperscaler contracts dampen spot-price volatility; leasing vs buying is the second mitigation. A sustained 50%+ price decline on H100 is the genuine break-case — it forces a generational pivot to Vera Rubin / next-gen, but the transformation-chain economics are robust to that pivot.

### 14. What if AI video ASP collapses?

Modelled at $0.005/sec (down 83% from $0.03 base case). The video line collapses from ~$2.4B annual revenue to ~$400M. Blended portfolio net drops ~19% but remains profitable; payback stays sub-1-year. Soft-foot rule limits concentration; the inference hardware is redeployable to image and 3D inference. AI video ASP is the most volatile leg of the book by design — that is why it's the smallest allocation (5 MW of 100 MW blended).

### 15. Single-product concentration rule?

Documented in §11 above. 30% soft-foot on physical asset exposure. The rule is reviewed quarterly by the trading desk and partnership function; breaches require either a rebalancing action or an explicit board approval with rationale logged. This is governance discipline, not a marketing claim.

### 16. Hedge policy?

Layered. **Layer 1 — physical hedge**: long-dated offtake contracts with credit-quality counterparties (hyperscalers, refiners) lock in volume + price floors. **Layer 2 — financial hedge**: commodity hedges (power, gas, CO2) on the merchant book; FX hedge on non-USD revenue. **Layer 3 — book hedge**: diversification across the transformation ladder so single-line shocks are absorbed by other legs. We do not run naked directional books on commodity price. Counterparty credit is margined where the counterparty allows.

### 17. Compliance posture?

Multi-jurisdictional. **EU ETS** compliance via monitored fossil-energy positions; allowance procurement and surrender handled in-house. **RED III** compliance for sustainable feedstock (SAF, eSAF, HVO) — feedstock chain-of-custody documentation maintained per shipment, audited annually. **CORSIA** for aviation fuel offtake — same chain-of-custody discipline. **UK ETS / CCA** for UK power and refining positions. **MAR / EMIR** for financial hedges where in scope. Compliance is not delegated; it is in the partnership and operations function.

## E. Team & execution

### 18. Org structure — who runs what?

Three functions, deliberately separated so the same person cannot both originate and approve it. **Trading desk** — market-making, hedges, book management. Reports to the CRO. **Partnership team** — strategic stakes, offtake origination, counterparty negotiation. Reports to the CCO. **Operations** — asset buildout, transformation, installation, asset management. Reports to the COO. **Applied AI** — proprietary intelligence layer (KCC). Reports to the CTO. **Finance / Risk** — control functions, dual-key on material movements. Each function is independently staffed; conflict-of-interest rules are logged.

### 19. Team / execution risk?

Nana Gyasi (CEO) brings 19 years of integrated commercial operations at ExxonMobil — aviation fuel, chemical, commercial road transport. Long-term supply contracts, volume structuring, counterparty risk management. Real execution, not research. Operators are hired **after** committed capital is in place — operators want to see cash committed before they commit themselves, and that sequence is rational. Product Directors (inverted-founders) lead each vertical once cash is committed. Headcount target Year 3: ~25–35 FTE across functions.

## F. Strategic partners

### 20. Geographic mix — UK / US / EU?

Deliberately split. **UK** as the legal and operational base; UK family offices as anchor LP base; UK assets for early commercialisation. **EU** (Estonia, Germany, Netherlands) for technology sourcing and asset deployment; EU regulatory familiarity (ETS, F-gases, RED III) is a deliberate advantage. **US** for hyperscaler compute and GPU supply. The mix is calibrated to limit single-jurisdiction regulatory shock (e.g., a UK planning change hits ~40% of assets, not 100%). US expansion pace depends on Series A outcome and is sequenced behind UK/EU stabilisation.

### 21. Partnership vs acquisition appetite?

**Partnership-first.** Kanay prefers strategic stakes + offtake + prepay structures (as in the Elcogen and Magnotherm examples) over full acquisition. Rationale: counterparty alignment is stronger when the founder retains operating responsibility; capital efficiency is higher when Kanay's cheque is minority equity + working-capital lines rather than full control. Acquisition is reserved for distressed-asset opportunities (e.g., stranded compute shells) or for capabilities Kanay cannot build through stakes (rare). The default is partnership.

### 22. IP defensibility?

Three layers. **Proprietary AI / classification (KCC)** — patents on the classification taxonomy and pricing models. Filed in UK + EU + US. **Process IP** — operational know-how on transformation steps (SOFC + compute integration, magnetic cooling at data-centre scale). Protected as trade secret, not patent. **Stake-based IP exposure** — through Elcogen, Magnotherm, Biomagnets equity stakes Kanay has visibility into upstream IP and can negotiate licences. KCC is the strongest single layer; the combination is the defensible product.

### 23. Family office / strategic LP mix?

Target LP base is **family offices + strategic LPs**, not institutional funds. Rationale: ticket size ($1–10M), duration (10+ years), and covenant flexibility match Kanay's asset duration and operating cadence. Institutional funds are too quarterly for a 25-year asset book. The Series A target raise of $25–50M is calibrated to family-office / strategic-LP capacity, with anchor commitments from 2–3 lead families. We are not building a fund-of-funds or pitching endowments for this round.

### 24. Exit pathways?

Three primary windows. **Compute REIT** — spin operating GPU clusters into a UK / US REIT; long-dated hyperscaler cash flows match REIT investor base. **Cooling / climate-tech roll-up** — bundle cooling + magnet stacks into a UK climate-tech platform. **Strategic secondary** — sale of Kanay's combined stake to a strategic acquirer (data-centre operator, infrastructure fund, integrated major). **IPO** is the long-tail option, sequenced behind a $500M+ annual revenue base; not the primary path.

## 25. Questions we ask ourselves (and how we answer them)

A candid section. The questions below are the ones the partnership team argues about internally; we surface them so investors see the same risks we do.

| Question | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Are we over-relying on AI video ASP? | Yes — that is why video is the smallest allocation (5 MW / 100 MW) and the soft-foot rule caps it. We will rebalance if it breaches 30% of book. |
| Can we actually recruit operators fast enough? | Risk is real. We de-risk it by committing capital first, then hiring operators; we target Year-3 headcount of 25–35 FTE, achievable but not guaranteed. Founders-network recruiting is the primary channel. |
| What if hyperscalers build their own compute? | They are — and they will. Our position is hyperscalers will always need 10–30% of their compute from third parties for capacity flexibility, peak load, and new-region buildout. We target that residual market, not the captive hyperscaler build. |
| Will GPU economics survive the next 18 months? | Unknown. Our position is: the transformation-chain economics hold under a range of GPU pricing because generation is the constant. The risk is concentration in a single GPU generation — which is why the portfolio mixes H100 (mature) and Vera Rubin (premium). |
